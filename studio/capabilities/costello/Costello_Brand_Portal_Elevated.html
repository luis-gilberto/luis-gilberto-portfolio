Chapter 02 Verbal Voice & Editorial Doctrine
Established
Powerful

Substance without bluster. Unshakable command of facts and statutory authority.

Precise

Exact procedural language. Zero throat-clearing, filler, or loose claims.

Protective

Shielding what matters: livelihoods, medical & legal licenses, and enterprise continuity.

Composed

Calm, measured, and in control when government pressure is highest.

Strategic

Anticipating how a step in one proceeding alters the trajectory of another.

Approved Writing Rules
  • Lead with the problem, stakes, strategy, or next decision.
  • Use active, plain language and strong, accurate verbs.
  • Name what is at risk when it helps the reader understand exposure.
  • Use short, controlled sentences where gravity calls for them.
  • Describe Costello's coordinated approach rather than criticizing other lawyers.
  • Frame representation around early coordination before proceedings gain momentum.
  • Demonstrate competence through exact analysis rather than self-congratulatory adjectives.
  • Rely on upright type, font-weight, or brand accents for emphasis; reserve italics strictly for direct verbatim quotes.
Retired Writing Habits
  • First-person founder voice in firm-level copy ("I" instead of "Costello Law Firm").
  • Using the acronym "CLF" in any public-facing copy.
  • Vague advertising clichés: "client-centered," "results-driven," "premier," "unmatched."
  • Empty reassurance or narrating feelings ("We know how scared you are").
  • Jargon, throat-clearing, casualness, humor, or self-deprecation.
  • Outcome guarantees or fixed resolution timelines (e.g., rigid "180-day guarantee").
  • Disparaging other attorneys or aggressive bravado ("The government knows we will take it to trial").
  • Italics for decorative emphasis, slogans, or headlines.

Chapter 05 Typography Hierarchy & Zero-Italics Doctrine
Established
Source Serif 4
Legal correspondence, pleadings, formal letterhead body, and chapter display titles. Conveys judicial authority and classical clarity.
Inter
Digital user interface, navigation tabs, buttons, forms, and general editorial reading text. High legibility across all screen densities.
JetBrains Mono
Metadata, timestamps, direct attorney telephone extensions, provenance tags, and technical specifications.
The Zero-Italics Doctrine: Italics are strictly prohibited for decorative emphasis, marketing slogans, or firm headlines. Emphasis is achieved through upright font-weight (`font-weight: 600`) or brand accent styling. Italics are reserved exclusively for direct verbatim client quotes and formal legal case citations.
