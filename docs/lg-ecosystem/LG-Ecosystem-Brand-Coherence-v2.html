The one thing to internalise

GenSpark did not have your brand. It had a screenshot of it. Everything visual in that package is a good-faith guess, and the guesses are close enough to feel authoritative, which is exactly what makes them dangerous. The strategy is worth keeping. The pixels are not.

Typography · four roles, two dialects

The family resemblance comes from a shared role system. The distinctiveness comes from which role each identity leans on. This is how two brands share DNA without wearing the same outfit.

RoleTokenLuis leansStudio leans
Editorial--font-editorialPrimary voice. Display, headlines, italic emphasisSparing. Endorsement line only

Why the strip is set in the utility role

Monospace at 10px reads as infrastructure. It cannot be mistaken for a brand voice, because neither identity uses it as one. The typeface itself enforces the rule that the strip is chrome, which is a more durable guarantee than a size limit.

Grid

Shared 12 column, 1320px maximum shell, 40px desktop gutters and 20px mobile. Strip and header share the shell so their left edges align to the content below.

Spacing

Shared 8px base. Scale runs 8 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 40 · 56 · 72 · 96. Every dimension in this document is a multiple of 4 and almost all are multiples of 8.

Motion

Shared cubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1). Micro interactions 180 to 300ms. Colour and opacity only. No scale, no translate, no bounce, on either property.

Photography

Luis: portrait, environmental, human presence, warm. Studio: architectural, spatial, negative space, material and light. Never stock operators at laptops on either.

Corners

Zero radius on all interactive elements across both identities. This is a shared discipline and one of the cheapest, most legible family signals available.

Hairlines

1px only, at 14 percent on paper and 16 percent on ink. Both properties use hairlines as structure rather than decoration. No shadows in chrome.

Why Studio sits 4px smaller

Optical, not hierarchical. A condensed architectural mark reads heavier than an editorial wordmark at the same measured height. The 28 to 24 relationship makes them feel equal in the header. If the real Studio mark turns out to be light or wide, adjust the number until they balance optically and record the new value here.

Four nodes is a wayfinding line. Five is a menu. The strip earns its restraint by staying at the number of things a person can read without scanning.

05b · Bilingual system

Two languages, one architecture

Both properties ship EN and ES. The toggle stays in the strip. Most bilingual sites fail on one detail: the toggle dumps you on the homepage instead of the page you were reading. That is the requirement to design against.

DecisionApproachWhy
URL structure/es/ subdirectory on both domainsWorks on static hosting, keeps domain authority in one place, trivially cacheable
DefaultEnglish at the root, x-default pointing thereOne canonical entry point
Toggle targetThe equivalent page, not the homepageEvery page declares its counterpart. If a translation is missing, the toggle hides rather than dead-ends
Auto redirectNever, based on Accept-LanguageBreaks caching and search indexing, and overrides a choice the person already made
PersistenceRemember the choice, apply it to internal links onlyHonours preference without hijacking a deliberate URL
Markuplang on <html>, reciprocal hreflang on every page pairNon negotiable for search and screen readers
TypographySame roles, same scale, both languagesSpanish is not a different brand
A measurement worth having before you build

The Spanish Studio navigation runs about eight percent wider than the English. Measuring it surfaced a bug that was already in the v2.0 spec: the Studio header overflowed below roughly 1024px in both languages, because the 900px breakpoint was set from the English strings alone. Studio nav is now 14px at a 26px gap, and the breakpoint moved to 1080px. Spanish clears it with room.

Copy that earns its length

Direct translation inflates chrome. "Get in touch" becomes "Conversemos", which is shorter and warmer than "Ponte en contacto". Write the Spanish nav as Spanish, not as translated English, and the width problem mostly solves itself.

Recheck with the real face

These numbers were measured with fallback metrics, so they are conservative. The real structural face is condensed and will run narrower. Re-measure with the actual font and the final Spanish strings, then tune the one breakpoint value rather than the layout.

08 · Transition

The strip holds. The page changes hands.

Crossing between properties should feel like turning a page in one publication, not opening a second tab. Continuity comes from the strip staying byte-identical. The signal of change is a single 4px square moving one position to the right.

What persists

Strip height, background, node order, node positions, type, tracking, gutter, shell width. Nothing about the strip is allowed to differ between properties.

What changes

The active marker moves. The surface scope inverts. The mark, the nav dialect and the CTA style change. That is the entire transition.

What must not happen

No cross-fade, no loading state, no animated scope inversion. A full page load across domains is honest. Dressing it up reads as a trick.

10 · Usage rules

When D3. When D1.

The failure mode to avoid is combining them so freely that your name appears three times on a Studio page. D1 is a tool for specific commercial moments, not a default garnish.

D3 · Minimal ecosystem

Canonical web architecture. Use everywhere online.

  • Every page of luis-gilberto.com
  • Every page of lgpractice.com
  • Insights articles on either property
  • The Hub and its tools
  • StrategyIQ product surfaces, wrapped by the Studio header

D1 · Endorsed practice

Situational. Use where authorship converts.

  • Proposals and statements of work
  • Sales, press and keynote decks
  • Case study covers and project credits
  • The Studio About page, once, in body content
  • The Studio footer, once
  • Invoices and contracts
The counting rule

On any single Studio page, "Luis Gilberto" appears in a branded position at most once, and that once is either the footer or the About page, never the header. The ecosystem strip node does not count against this, because the strip is chrome. If you can see your name twice in one viewport on lgpractice.com, something is wrong.

11 · Do and don't

The rules that keep this from drifting

Do

  • Let exactly one identity own each page, at full strength, with room to breathe.
  • Keep the ecosystem strip identical on both properties, down to node order and pixel height.
  • Bind every colour and typeface to a canonical token rather than a literal value.
  • Let the two identities speak different typographic dialects. The resemblance lives in the system.
  • Drop the real marks into the defined slots and adjust heights until they balance optically.
  • Keep the accent to one appearance per view.
  • Keep destinations one tier below identities everywhere they appear together.
  • Ship the strip as inlined critical CSS in both codebases.

Don't

  • Never stack two full-strength branded headers. This is the single rule the whole system exists to enforce.
  • Never place a Luis Gilberto lockup above, beside or beneath the Studio mark in site chrome.
  • Never let the ecosystem strip grow past 34px or gain a logo, a search field or a dropdown.
  • Never reintroduce "Portfolio" to the personal lockup.
  • Never let the endorsement line grow past its ceiling. It stops being a credit and becomes a second brand.
  • Never style Insights or The Hub as an identity.
  • Never force both marks into one monogram or one geometry.
  • Never use lgstudio.com, or rename LG Studio to LG Practice.
