Edge Mobile App Store Rebrand

Platform-native optimization that made Edge discoverable and compelling in iOS and Android app stores. Turned invisible into inevitable.

Microsoft Edge • 2023 • 4 Months

Project Details

  • Role Mobile Strategy Lead
  • Duration 4 Months
  • Platforms iOS & Android
  • Markets 15+ Global

The problem wasn't the product. Edge mobile had the features, the speed, the integration. But none of that mattered if users couldn't find it—or didn't care when they did.

Microsoft Edge was invisible in app stores. Buried in search results. Generic in presentation. And when users actually discovered it, the conversion rate was abysmal. The challenge wasn't building a better browser—it was making people believe they needed one.

Mobile app stores operate on split-second decisions. Visual impact. Social proof. Immediate value perception. Edge's presence screamed "desktop software trying to be mobile," and users scrolled right past.

Platform-First Strategy

We stopped trying to force Edge into both app stores the same way. iOS users weren't Android users. Their behaviors, priorities, and expectations were fundamentally different. Privacy mattered more on iOS. Customization won on Android. One-size-fits-all was a recipe for mediocrity.

The strategy centered on platform-native optimization while maintaining Edge brand coherence. iOS got Apple design language, privacy messaging, ecosystem integration emphasis. Android got Material Design alignment, customization features, performance optimization focus.

Keyword research revealed the gap between what Edge offered and what users searched for. We weren't ranking for high-intent terms. Competitors owned "private browser," "fast browser," "secure browser." We needed to claim territory in the mobile browser search landscape.

Visual Identity Evolution

App icons got rebuilt from scratch—maintaining Edge branding while improving visibility in crowded app store grids. Screenshots stopped being feature lists and became visual stories. Privacy tools in action. Performance benchmarks with context. Ecosystem integration showing real value.

Messaging shifted from technical specifications to user benefits. "Sync across devices" became "Pick up where you left off." "Tracking prevention" became "Browse without being followed." Every word had to justify its space in the limited character counts of app store descriptions.

A/B testing revealed what worked: Progressive disclosure won over information dumps. Social proof (ratings, reviews) mattered more than feature counts. Visual consistency across screenshots built trust faster than variety.

The Results

Downloads jumped 185% on average across platforms. iOS saw 165% improvement, Android 205%. But the numbers that mattered more: conversion rates up 220%, user acquisition costs down 65%, organic downloads up 300%.

Edge climbed to top 5 in iOS search rankings for key browser terms. Android search visibility improved 75%. App ratings stabilized at 4.6 stars combined—high enough to build trust, real enough to feel authentic.

The rebrand's impact extended beyond metrics. It established a framework for how Microsoft approached mobile app optimization across its portfolio. Platform-specific beats brand-uniform when you're competing for attention in seconds.

App stores don't reward the best product. They reward the best presentation of value in the shortest time. Edge became both.

The Impact

185% increase in download rates across iOS and Android. Platform-native design turned invisible into inevitable.

✓ What Worked

  • Platform-native design
    iOS and Android users respond to platform conventions, not brand uniformity
  • Visual storytelling
    Screenshots showing value in action beat feature specification lists
  • Progressive disclosure
    Lead with benefits, layer in details for users who want them

✗ What Didn't

  • Cross-platform uniformity
    Same assets on both platforms performed worse than platform-specific
  • Feature-led messaging
    Users needed to see value, not read specifications
  • Set-and-forget optimization
    App store algorithms change constantly—ongoing iteration required
