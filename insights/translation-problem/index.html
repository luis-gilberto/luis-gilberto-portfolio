The Hidden Anti-Pattern: Humans are APIs

For all the digital swagger,

most organizations run on one very analog integration.

Humans are APIs
Carol from accounting Remembers what was really decided when the deck says something else entirely.
Tom’s notebook Holds the real logic on paper because the roadmap lives in a tool no one checks.
Why this is brittle Carol and Tom are not a system.
They are load-bearing infrastructure pretending to be one.
It works until Carol takes vacation.
Until Tom pivots.
Until someone is “reallocated.”
Execution becomes dangerously fragile by design.

People are essential.

People are not process.

Blame assignment, meanwhile, remains remarkably stable.

Diagnosis:

Skill is not lacking.

Structure is.

Decks: Where Strategy Goes to Take a Nap

Slide decks are legendary for two reasons.

Act 1: They helped you Win the Meeting.

Act 2: They murdered everything after.

Decks are where strategy goes to hibernate.

What Translation Failure Actually Looks Like

  • Priorities “shift” every Monday.
  • Like civic weather.
  • No decisive meeting notes.
  • Only confident recollections.
  • Status updates: ninety percent interpretation.
  • Ten percent hope.
  • Ownership as stable as startup valuations.
  • After attrition: nobody knows what remains, but everyone agrees it was important.

Translation is not magic.

It is infrastructure.

Most places treat it like drizzle.

The gap was never strategy.

It was always the space in between.

Systems built for translation carry what gets decided.

Into what actually gets done.

They survive context shifts.

They survive calendar flips.

They survive the departure of Carol.

Name the gap.

Build the bridge.

Watch what changes.

The work is not more strategy. It is better translation.
