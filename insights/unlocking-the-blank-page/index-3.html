Unlocking the Blank Page

How I used AI to remember my own voice.

November 2025 • 7 min read

The Cell
of Fear

Every creative knows this moment. It isn't the absence of ideas that holds you back. It's their weight.

The blank page isn't neutral. It's a dare.

It started with panic.

The realization that my résumé hadn't been updated in years, and that every artifact of my work was scattered across time, platforms, and tools, hit me like a wave. I had memory, sure. But memory is slippery.

And there I was, facing the most intimidating creative opponent of all: a blinking cursor.

This wasn't just about updating a résumé. I wanted something bigger, maybe even audacious: a living time capsule. Something that didn't just list jobs and dates but captured the rollercoaster of campaigns, launches, late nights, and reinventions that shaped me.

But ambition has a way of making the blank page feel even heavier.

The Key

My memories and stories pressed at the edges of my mind like prisoners gripping the bars of a cell, waiting to break free. I wasn't afraid of silence. I was afraid of not knowing how to begin.

That's when AI stepped in. Not as a ghostwriter. Not as a stand-in. As a key.

It gave me the scaffolding I needed. A frame to build on. A nudge to move past the paralysis of the first line. It wasn't about perfection. It was about momentum. And momentum brought freedom.

AI With Soul

Suddenly, the stories began to pour. Not because AI wrote them, but because it caught them as I poured them out.

It helped me shape fragments I'd neglected, amplify sparks I'd left dormant. It unlocked more than words. It unlocked my passion for writing again.

Here's what people get wrong: they think using AI means handing over authorship. But that's not how it works, at least not when you use it right.

I didn't feed AI a prompt and walk away. I gave it pieces of me—fragments, memories, half-formed ideas—and watched it help me see patterns I couldn't see on my own. It was like having a thought partner who never gets tired, never judges, and always says, "What if you tried this?"

Why It Matters

We're living through a moment where AI feels both revolutionary and terrifying. Some people see it as a shortcut. Others see it as a threat. I see it as a tool, one that amplifies intention.

The blank page isn't scary because we lack ideas. It's scary because we lack permission to be imperfect. AI gave me that permission. It let me write badly first, then refine. It let me think out loud without judgment. It let me remember that creation is messy, nonlinear, and deeply human.

That's what I mean by "AI with soul."

It's not about replacing the human touch. It's about using technology to amplify it. To make space for curiosity. To unlock what was already there, waiting.

I didn't need AI to write for me. I needed it to sit with me. To help me get the first word out. To loosen whatever had calcified between my mind and my hands.

It didn't replace my voice. It reminded me I still had one.

The Problem

The blank page asks what you believe, what you see, and whether you will bet time and credibility on a shape that does not exist yet.

The Truth

The voice?
Mine.

The stories?
Mine.

The choices?
Mine.

AI was the key that unlocked the door. But I'm the one who walked through it.

This is me, writing again. Alive in the work. Present at the page.
