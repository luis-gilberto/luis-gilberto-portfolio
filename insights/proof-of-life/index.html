Proof of Life

Being awake in the work you build.

Human × Tech

This isn't about building websites. This is about creative presence—showing up in the work you make, leaving fingerprints, proving you weren't on autopilot.

Not output. Not performance. Proof that you were awake.

The Problem with Perfect

Most brand websites feel like nobody made them. They're optimized, templated, and safe—designed by committee, written by AI, approved by legal.

They perform. They convert. They don't breathe.

And that's fine if you're selling widgets. But if you're selling you—your thinking, your taste, your vision—then generic doesn't work. You need proof of life.

What Proof of Life Actually Means

It's not about perfection. It's about presence:

A choice that only you would make.
The Big Shoulders Display font at 6rem. The coral gradient. The manifesto-style signature lines. These aren't best practices—they're fingerprints.

Language that sounds like a human wrote it.
No "solutions-driven strategic thought leader" nonsense. Just sentences with rhythm, voice, and a point of view.

Design that takes a position.
Black gradients. Oversized type. Generous white space. This isn't minimal—it's intentional.

"The opposite of generic isn't weird. It's specific."

When someone lands on this site, they should know instantly that a person made it. Not a template. Not an agency production line. You.

Building as Creative Practice

This is the part nobody talks about: building your own site is creative work.

Not in the "I dabble in code" way. In the "I'm making decisions about hierarchy, pacing, emphasis, voice" way. The same decisions you make in a campaign brief or brand manifesto.

You're not just arranging divs. You're designing an argument:

  • → How much context before the work?
  • → What size should the headline be?
  • → Where does the eye go first?
  • → What's the last thing they remember?

Every spacing decision, every font weight, every word—it's all information architecture for your brain.

And when you build it yourself, you control the whole stack. The content. The design. The argument.

Why This Matters for Creatives

Because templated portfolios don't differentiate anymore. Everyone's using the same Webflow templates, the same Notion embeds, the same grid layouts.

Your site is the first project people see. If it looks like everyone else's, why would they expect your work to be different?

This isn't about learning to code. It's about controlling the first impression. Making sure that before someone reads a single case study, they already know:

  • → You have taste
  • → You make deliberate choices
  • → You care about craft

That's what proof of life does. It's not a bio section or an About page. It's the whole experience.

Not just what you made.
How you made it.
And why anyone should care.

The Ecosystem

This site isn't alone. It's part of a bigger system:

The Hub – Product offering. Where clients go to explore services, see pricing, book time.
Utility-first. Clean. Transactional.

Insights – Editorial platform. Where the thinking lives. Case studies, essays, frameworks.
Content-rich. Designed for depth.

Luis-Gilberto.com – The portfolio. The proof. The introduction.
Opinionated. Personal. Alive.

Three sites. One system. Each with a specific job.

And all of it built the same way: intentionally, manually, with fingerprints.

Final Thought

Proof of life isn't a design trend. It's a signal.

It says: I was here. I made choices. This is mine.

In a world of templates, AI slop, and design-by-consensus, that's everything.

"Show them you were awake."
