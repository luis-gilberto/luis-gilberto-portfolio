            <h1>Unlocking the Blank Page</h1>
            <p class="article-subtitle">How I used AI to remember my own voice.</p>

            <div class="article-byline-row">
                <span>By Luis Gilberto</span>
                <span class="byline-sep">/</span>

        <article class="main-content">
            
            <div class="reading-content" id="intro">
                <p class="lead"><strong>It started with panic.</strong> A specific kind of digital paralysis. The cursor blinking on the empty white screen wasn't an invitation; it was an accusation.</p>

                <p>For years, I wrote for algorithms. I wrote for SEO scores, for click-through rates, for engagement metrics. I had become a machine for generating content that machines liked. And in the process, I lost the ability to just... write.</p>

                <p>When I sat down to write something personal—something real—I froze. I was waiting for the optimization tool to tell me my headline was too long, or my keyword density was too low.</p>

                <div class="inset-card" style="border-left-color: var(--coral-accent);">
                    <h4>The Problem</h4>
                    <p>I had forgotten how to write without an editor. Not a human one—the algorithmic one living in my head. I needed to break the loop.</p>
                </div>
            </div>

            <div class="reading-content" id="mirror">
                <h3>The AI Mirror</h3>
                <p>I decided to use ChatGPT. Not to write for me, but to talk to me. To be the mirror I couldn't find in the metrics dashboards.</p>
                <p>I started treating the AI not as a generator, but as a provocateur. I asked it to challenge my premises. I asked it to ask <em>me</em> questions. "Why do you think this matters?" it would ask. "What are you afraid to say here?"</p>
                <p>It was jarring. But it was effective. By arguing with a machine, I found the friction I needed to spark my own thoughts.</p>
            </div>

            <div class="reading-content" id="process">
                <h3>The Process</h3>
                <p>It wasn't about copy-pasting. It was about dialogue. The blank page wasn't blank anymore; it was a conversation space.</p>

                <div class="inset-card">
                    <h4>What Worked</h4>
                    <ul>
                        <li><strong>Structure, not content:</strong> "Give me 3 counter-intuitive ways to look at this."</li>
                        <li><strong>Voice-to-text:</strong> "Talking out" the draft before typing.</li>
                        <li><strong>Devil's Advocate:</strong> Asking for opposing views to sharpen my arguments.</li>
                    </ul>
                </div>

                <div class="inset-card">
                    <h4>What I Left Behind</h4>
                    <ul>
                        <li>SEO keyword stuffing as a first step.</li>
                        <li>Generic "In this article, we will explore..." intros.</li>
                        <li>Letting the tool have the final say.</li>
                    </ul>
                </div>

                <p>Gradually, the panic subsided. The cursor stopped accusing and started waiting. I realized that the AI wasn't doing the work; it was clearing the noise so <em>I</em> could do the work.</p>
            </div>

            <div class="reading-content" id="truth">
                <div class="quote-card" style="margin-top: 4rem; padding: 2.5rem; background: var(--bg-secondary); border-radius: 12px; text-align: center;">
                    <p style="font-family: 'Big Shoulders Display'; font-size: 2rem; line-height: 1.2; color: var(--text-primary); margin-bottom: 1rem;">The Truth: AI didn't replace my voice. It forced me to find it again.</p>
                </div>
            </div>
