1. The Specs (Send to Gen Spark/Trae)
Since the user clicks "Play," we can afford a larger file size to ensure quality.
Format: .mp4 (H.264 Codec).
Resolution: 1920x1080 (1080p).

Crucial: Ensure the mix is normalized to -14 LUFS (standard web volume) so it doesn't blast the user's ears compared to other content.

This ensures no "blocky" artifacts during the fast-motion scenes.
File Size Goal: Aim for under 50MB for a 35-second clip.

        <div class="reading-content" id="intro">
            <p class="lead"><strong>Browser marketing is usually boring. We wanted to change that.</strong></p>
            <p>Most tech companies treat support content as an afterthought. It lives in dusty FAQs or dry help articles. But we noticed a pattern: people weren't reading the help docs. They were searching YouTube for answers and finding nothing.</p>
            <p>We saw an opportunity to turn "Support" into "Discovery." But we didn't want to make tutorials. Tutorials are about clicking buttons. We wanted to make <strong>Lifestyle Proof-of-Concepts</strong>.</p>
            
            <div class="inset-card" style="border-left-color: var(--coral-accent);">
                <h4>The Insight</h4>
                <p>If they are asking how to do it, they want to use it. Confusion isn't a barrier; it's a signal of intent.</p>

            <div class="visual-break">

        <div class="reading-content" id="formula">
            <h3>The Narrative Formula</h3>
            <p>Each video followed a tight, 30-40 second structure designed to respect the user's intelligence.</p>
            <p><strong>1. The Hook:</strong> A relatable scenario ("How to party with Edge"). Not a feature name.</p>
            <p><strong>2. Context Setup:</strong> A human host introduces a real-world goal. "I need to plan a birthday."</p>
            <p><strong>3. Feature Demo:</strong> A rapid-fire walkthrough. We didn't show the mouse moving across the screen; we showed the *result* of the action.</p>
            
            <div class="inset-card">
                <h4>The Human Bookend</h4>
                <p>The host appears at the start and finish, never in the middle. The face builds trust; the UI builds competence.</p>
            </div>
        </div>

        <div class="reading-content" id="visuals">
            <h3>Visual Style: Deconstructed UI</h3>
            <p>This wasn't screen recording. It was <strong>UI as Hero Object</strong>. We "lifted" the Edge browser out of the OS and placed it in a stylized 3D environment.</p>
            <p>We removed the messiness of real browsing, including the ads, the 50 open tabs, and the toolbar clutter, to isolate exactly what the viewer needed to learn. It wasn't deceptive; it was aspirational. It showed the software as it feels when you're in flow.</p>
        </div>

        <div class="reading-content" id="money-shot">
            <h3>The Money Shot</h3>
            <p>Every script built toward a critical payoff: the transition from <strong>Digital to Physical.</strong></p>
            <p>The screen would dissolve into the real-world outcome: the balloons at the party, the packed suitcase, the finished meal. This was the proof. The software didn't just help you browse; it helped you <em>do</em>.</p>

            <div class="visual-break">

            </div>

                <p class="large-quote">We don't teach features. We show outcomes.</p>

            <p class="closing-paragraph">That's the power of building a system, not just a video.</p>
