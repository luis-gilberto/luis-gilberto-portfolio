Account Overview

Your Account

Posting for: 5 years

Followers: 26.9k

Brand Description

Criar Sin Culpas is for parents who are overwhelmed and struggling to regulate themselves or their children—especially in moments of escalation. I speak to families raising disruptive, aggressive, or neurodivergent kids, including those on the spectrum, and also to parents who may not yet realize their child needs additional support or early intervention.

This space addresses what's actually happening at home: yelling, disconnection, lack of structure—and yes, parents who sometimes scream or hit because they feel out of control.

I combine real-life experience with practical, evidence-based tools like PCIT and the RUBI curriculum to help parents de-escalate, communicate effectively, and build structure that works in real life.

What Makes You Different

What makes Criar Sin Culpas different is that it's a safe place for honesty without judgment—and with real guidance.

Parents don't have to pretend everything is fine. They can say what's actually happening at home—yelling, hitting, losing control, kids escalating—and know they won't be shamed for it.

But it's not just a safe space to vent.

It's a space where they receive clear, practical, and evidence-based support on what to do instead. I don't normalize staying stuck—I help parents understand what's happening, de-escalate in real time, and build structure that actually works.

Ideal Follower

My ideal follower is a parent raising a child who is intense, disruptive, or neurodivergent—often dealing with aggression, defiance, or constant escalation at home.

They're overwhelmed and dysregulated themselves. They yell, they lose control, and yes—sometimes they hit. Not because they want to, but because they don't know what else to do in the moment.

There's little structure, communication isn't working, and they feel like nothing they try sticks. In some cases, they suspect something deeper is going on with their child—or they haven't yet realized it, but know something isn't right.

They feel judged, isolated, and exhausted.

Age range: 18–24, 25–34, 35–44, 45–54

Common Questions

They reach out in real time, when everything is already escalating.

  • "My child is hitting/screaming—what do I do right now?"
  • "I'm losing control—how do I not react?"
  • "Is it getting worse—how do I de-escalate it?"

Most of their questions come when everything is falling apart in the moment—they need immediate, practical guidance to handle the situation without making it worse.
