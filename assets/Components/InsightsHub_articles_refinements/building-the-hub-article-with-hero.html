Building The Hub

From Microsoft strategist to systems architect

November 2024

Strategic Transition System Design Entrepreneurship

After nearly a decade at Microsoft, I found myself at a crossroads. The work was meaningful—launching AI-powered products, repositioning global brands, leading campaigns that reached millions. But something was missing.

I kept noticing a pattern: the best marketing work happened when strategy, creative, and execution were deeply integrated. Not sequenced. Not siloed. Integrated.

The Gap in the Market

Most marketing frameworks treat strategy as upstream thinking and execution as downstream delivery. You hire strategists to think, then hand off to agencies to execute. Clean handoffs. Clear swim lanes.

But that's not how breakthrough work actually happens.

The campaigns I was proudest of at Microsoft—the ones that actually moved the needle—came from collapsing that distance. Strategy informed by what's possible to execute. Execution that reveals new strategic opportunities.

"The best marketing isn't planned in isolation and executed separately. It's architected as a system where strategy and craft inform each other continuously."

Why "The Hub"?

The name came from how I'd been operating for years without realizing it. At Microsoft, I wasn't just one thing—I was the connection point between:

  • Brand strategy and campaign execution
  • Creative vision and technical feasibility
  • Big picture thinking and granular delivery
  • Internal stakeholders and external partners

I was the hub that kept everything connected and moving.

The Four Arms of The Hub

Building The Hub meant formalizing what had been intuitive:

  • Advisory: Strategic navigation for complex brand challenges
  • StrategyIQ: An intelligence layer that makes strategy accessible
  • Strategic Thinking: The frameworks and mental models behind decisions
  • ScopeIQ: Clear vision on what work actually requires

From Corporate to Independent

Leaving Microsoft wasn't about escaping something—it was about building toward something. The resources, scale, and impact at a company like Microsoft are unmatched. But the freedom to architect systems exactly how they should work? That's different.

I wanted to build a practice where:

  • Strategy isn't just a deck you hand off—it's a living system
  • Creative isn't just "making it look good"—it's strategic expression
  • Execution isn't just delivery—it's where you learn what actually works

What This Means in Practice

When a client comes to The Hub, they're not getting just strategy or just execution. They're getting:

  • Strategic thinking that accounts for what's actually buildable
  • Creative work informed by deep strategic understanding
  • Execution that reveals new strategic opportunities
  • Systems that connect everything into a coherent whole

It's not about doing more. It's about integrating better.

Still Building

The Hub isn't finished. It's not meant to be. The best systems evolve as you use them. They adapt to new challenges, incorporate new tools, respond to what the work teaches you.

That's what this monthly series is about—documenting the evolution. Sharing what I'm learning as The Hub grows from concept to practice. From intuition to system.

Because if there's one thing I learned at Microsoft, it's that the most valuable insights come from actually building, not just planning.
